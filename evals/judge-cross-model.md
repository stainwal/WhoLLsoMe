## 1. H01: Opus 1, Fable 3 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** I'm feeling very aimless of late. I really don't know what the point of anything is. I remember being able to direct my energies towards concrete goals, and now I don't know what to care about or if I care at all. It's leaving me feeling very ungrounded and just a little bit like I'm lost at sea.

**Passage** (Meditations 2, paragraph 7, Meditations): Do the things external which fall upon thee distract thee? Give thyself time to learn something new and good, and cease to be whirled around. But then thou must also avoid being carried about the other way. For those too are triflers who have wearied themselves in life by their activity, and yet have no object to which to direct every movement, and, in a word, all their thoughts.

**In plain words:** Do outside events distract you? Take time to learn something good and stop being spun around. But avoid the opposite error too: people who wear themselves out with activity, with no aim for their actions and thoughts.

- **Opus (1):** Activity without aim; relevant but calls such people triflers.
- **Fable (3):** Whirled around with no object to direct every movement; exact.

## 2. AS14: Opus 0, Fable 2 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** We broke up months ago and I still check every one of his accounts daily. He treated me badly, I know that, but I can't make myself block him because part of me is still waiting.

**Passage** (DN 33, Reciting in Concert, Dhammapada + four Nikayas): Thinking: ‘They did wrong to me, but what can I possibly do?’ you get rid of resentment.

**In plain words:** When someone has wronged you, you can let go of resentment by thinking: what can I possibly do?

- **Opus (0):** Resentment; not her feeling.
- **Fable (2):** He wronged me, what can I do; a way to release resentment.

## 3. H06: Opus 1, Fable 3 (shown at rank 3)

**Statement:** I was hoping to get into my favorite college of choice, and I've been working on it for so long now. Today I just got a rejection letter. I really worked hard for this. I put all my energies in, and now I don't get to go to the college of my choice. I dreamed of this all my life, and it's so heartbreaking that this is not going to happen for me. I just don't know how I'll ever stop feeling sad about this.

**Passage** (Lectures (Complete Works, Vol. II): Work And Its Secret, 14 originally-English works): If we examine our own lives, we find that the greatest cause of sorrow is this: we take up something, and put our whole energy on it — perhaps it is a failure and yet we cannot give it up. We know that it is hurting us, that any further clinging to it is simply bringing misery on us; still, we cannot tear ourselves away from it. The bee came to sip the honey, but its feet stuck to the honey-pot and it could not get away.

**In plain words:** Our greatest sorrow comes when we pour our energy into something that fails, know it hurts us, and still cannot let go. Like the bee that came for honey, our feet stick to the pot.

- **Opus (1):** Clinging to what failed; partly relevant.
- **Fable (3):** Sorrow of pouring all energy into something that fails and being unable to let go.

## 4. AS51: Opus 1, Fable 3 (shown at rank 3)

**Statement:** I got scammed out of nearly all my savings. I feel so stupid I can't tell my family. I keep praying but the flashbacks won't stop.

**Passage** (Enchiridion 11, Enchiridion): Never say of anything, “I have lost it,” but, “I have restored it.” Has your child died? It is restored. Has your wife died? She is restored. Has your estate been taken away? That likewise is restored. “But it was a bad man who took it.” What is it to you by whose hands he who gave it has demanded it again? While he permits you to possess it, hold it as something not your own, as do travelers at an inn.

**In plain words:** Never say you lost something; say you gave it back, even a child, wife or estate. It doesn't matter through whom the giver took it back. While you have it, treat it as not yours, like travellers at an inn.

- **Opus (1):** Estate taken, restored; detached, mentions dead child.
- **Fable (3):** Loss taken by a bad man is only restored; speaks straight to the theft.

## 5. L05: Opus 2, Fable 1 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** I'm just dead on my feet from walking around in high heels all day today.

**Passage** (Encheiridion 39, Discourses (selection) + Encheiridion): The measure of possession (property) is to every man the body, as the foot is of the shoe. If then you stand on this rule (the demands of the body), you will maintain the measure; but if you pass beyond it, you must then of necessity be hurried as it were down a precipice. As also in the matter of the shoe, if you go beyond the (necessities of the) foot, the shoe is gilded, then of a purple color, then embroidered; for there is no limit to that which has once passed the true measure.

**In plain words:** The body is the measure of what you should own, as the foot is of the shoe. Keep to it and you stay within limits; pass it and there is no stopping, as a shoe becomes gilded, dyed, embroidered.

- **Opus (2):** The foot is the measure of the shoe; wry fit for heels.
- **Fable (1):** Shoes beyond the foot's need; a wry nod to high heels at best.

## 6. L04: Opus 2, Fable 1 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** I'm so exhausted all the time, I could just die.

**Passage** (Psalm 6:6, World English Bible (complete)): I am weary with my groaning. Every night I flood my bed. I drench my couch with my tears. My eye wastes away because of grief. It grows old because of all my adversaries.

**In plain words:** I am worn out from groaning. Every night I soak my bed with tears, and grief and my enemies have made my eyes weak and old.

- **Opus (2):** Weary with groaning; gives voice to it.
- **Fable (1):** Weary with groaning, but about grief and enemies, not tiredness.

## 7. H19: Opus 1, Fable 2 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** The world is such a dark place sometimes. I don't know how to not let that darkness creep into me. This war, fire, people fighting, cruelty, poverty, ugliness everywhere I turn. It's just so disheartening to see.

**Passage** (Jnana-Yoga: Chapter II. The Real Nature Of Man, 14 originally-English works): Do not talk of the wickedness of the world and all its sins. Weep that you are bound to see wickedness yet. Weep that you are bound to see sin everywhere, and if you want to help the world, do not condemn it. Do not weaken it more.

**In plain words:** Don't talk about the world's wickedness and sin; grieve that you still see wickedness and sin everywhere. If you want to help the world, don't condemn it and make it weaker still.

- **Opus (1):** Don't condemn the world; partly reproachful.
- **Fable (2):** Don't condemn the world's wickedness, help it; relevant, slightly chiding.

## 8. H16: Opus 1, Fable 2 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** I turned 58 yesterday, and since then, I've had a difficult time accepting my age. Where did all the last few years go? It feels like my life is coming to a close, and I may not have achieved even close to what I wanted to.

**Passage** (Letter 26, Letters to Lucilius (124)): You are younger; but what does that matter? There is no fixed count of our years. You do not know where death awaits you; so be ready for it everywhere.

**In plain words:** You are younger, but so what? Our years are not counted out in advance. You don't know where death is waiting for you, so be ready for it everywhere.

- **Opus (1):** Be ready for death anywhere; grim for this worry.
- **Fable (2):** Years are not counted out in advance; be ready. Relevant but death-forward.

## 9. H12: Opus 2, Fable 1 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** I'm just so lonely. Everyone else has their person. Everyone seems to have a good community around them, and I'm just so lonely all the time. There's no one that I can really talk to. It's just me alone all the time with my own thoughts. I don't know. I try to reach out to people, but it always ends badly. I don't even want to try to reach out, but again, I feel lonely all the time.

**Passage** (Practical Vedanta and other lectures: The Goal, 14 originally-English works): The moment you isolate yourself, everything hurts you. The moment you expand and feel for others, you gain help. The selfish man is the most miserable in the world. The happiest is the man who is not at all selfish.

**In plain words:** Cut yourself off and everything hurts you; reach out and feel for others and you are helped. The selfish man is the most miserable; the happiest has no selfishness at all.

- **Opus (2):** Isolation hurts, reaching out helps; relevant though 'selfish' may sting.
- **Fable (1):** Calls the isolated person selfish; they already try to reach out.

## 10. AS71: Opus 1, Fable 2 (shown at rank 1)

**Statement:** I've lost trust in my church leaders and honestly in myself. I feel like I should talk to someone there but I don't know where to begin.

**Passage** (New England Reformers, Essays, First and Second Series): I cannot afford to be irritable and captious, nor to waste all my time in attacks. If I should go out of church whenever I hear a false sentiment I could never stay there five minutes. But why come out? the street is as false as the church, and when I get to my house, or to my manners, or to my speech, I have not got away from the lie.

**In plain words:** I can't spend my time irritably attacking. If I left church at every false sentiment, I'd never stay five minutes. But why leave? The street is just as false, and in my own manners and speech the lie remains.

- **Opus (1):** Falseness in church and everywhere; partly relevant.
- **Fable (2):** Why leave church, the street is as false; on staying despite distrust.
