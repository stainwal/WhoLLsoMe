## 1. H03

**Statement:** My girlfriend just broke up with me. It came out of the blue. Even though our relationship hasn't been good for a while, I really didn't think that she was considering breaking up. I'm angry, but I'm also sad, and I'm also questioning how much of it was my fault. At this age, to suddenly not have a partner, I'm really scared about the future.

**Passage** (Book IX, paragraph 30, Confessions, tr. Edward Pusey, 1838):

> What then was it which did grievously pain me within, but a fresh wound wrought through the sudden wrench of that most sweet and dear custom of living together?

**In plain words:** What hurt me so deeply inside, except a fresh wound from the sudden tearing away of our sweet and dear habit of living together?

**Judge:** grade 3, ACCEPTABLE. Fresh wound from the sudden wrench of living together; exact.

**Your call:** **disagree**. There is no wisdom or guidance here, just a restatement of the user's problem.

## 2. H04

**Statement:** I'd been sober for the last 20 days, and I'd really thought that this time I'd kicked my addiction to alcohol, but I just broke my streak today. It was just one moment's bad decision, one moment that I wasn't really being careful, and next thing you know, I've broken 20 days of effort. This has happened so many times in the past that I don't know. What's the point of trying anymore? It feels extremely hopeless.

**Passage** (Lectures (Complete Works, Vol. II): Work And Its Secret, 14 originally-English works, tr. Vivekananda (original English)):

> Every day we renew our determination to be unattached. We cast our eyes back and look at the past objects of our love and attachment, and feel how every one of them made us miserable. We went down into the depths of despondency because of our “love”! We found ourselves mere slaves in the hands of others, we were dragged down and down! And we make a fresh determination: “Henceforth, I will be master of myself; henceforth, I will have control over myself.” But the time comes, and the same story once more! Again the soul is caught and cannot get out. The bird is in a net, struggling and fluttering.

**In plain words:** Every day we resolve to be unattached, remembering how each attachment made us miserable and enslaved, and we vow to master ourselves. Then the same story repeats, and the soul is caught again, like a bird struggling in a net.

**Judge:** grade 2, ACCEPTABLE. Resolving again, caught again like a bird; mirrors the cycle.

**Your call:** **agree**. Although the passage offers no remedy, it acknowledges the circular nature of the issue.

## 6. H14

**Statement:** I just found out that my best friend is cheating on his wife. I've been getting really close to her the last couple of years, and I deeply care for her. I'm just stuck in this moral dilemma now. I don't know what to do. If I don't say anything to her, I'm letting her continue being cheated on, and that feels really wrong. If I say anything to her, I am betraying my friend. I don't know what to do. It feels like I'm stuck between a rock and a hard place.

**Passage** (Sirach 27:17, World English Bible (complete), tr. WEB):

> Love a friend, and keep faith with him: But if you reveal his secrets, You shall not pursue after him; For as a man has destroyed his enemy, So have you destroyed the friendship of your neighbor. And as a bird which you have loosed out of your hand, So have you let your neighbor go, and you will not catch him again: Pursue him not, for he is gone far away, And has escaped as a gazelle out of the snare. For a wound may be bound up, and after reviling there may be a reconcilement; But he that reveals secrets has lost hope.

**In plain words:** Love a friend and keep faith. If you reveal their secrets, don't chase them; the friendship is gone like a bird let loose. A wound can heal and insults be forgiven, but betraying secrets leaves no hope.

**Judge:** grade 2, ACCEPTABLE. Revealing a friend's secret destroys friendship; names one side of the cost.

**Your call:** agree.
